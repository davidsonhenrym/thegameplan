# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Serious college sports fans and industry people — coaches, analysts, athletic department staff, and deeply engaged fans who follow recruiting and roster construction closely. They read analytically, not casually. They're skimming for signal, not entertainment. Many are on a laptop in a quiet office or at home late at night, already thinking about the next decision. They have low tolerance for filler and high respect for honest, rigorous takes.

## Product Purpose

The Game Plan is an independent editorial site publishing analytical deep dives into the changing NCAA landscape: the transfer portal economy, NIL markets, the House v. NCAA revenue sharing settlement, conference realignment, and competitive balance. It exists to produce honest, data-grounded analysis of the structural forces reshaping college sports — not hot takes, not news aggregation. Success is a piece that makes a coach or a serious fan think differently about something they thought they already understood.

## Brand Personality

Sharp. Independent. Curious.

Voice is confident but never arrogant — it makes calls and backs them up with numbers. Never hedges just to be safe. Treats the reader as a peer, not a student. No hype, no sensationalism.

## Anti-references

- Generic blog templates — no WordPress vibes, no centered body text, no stock photo headers, no visual noise
- ESPN / mainstream sports media — no chyrons, no hot-take framing, no loud primary-color branding
- VC-funded startup SaaS pages — no gradient heroes, feature bullet grids, or pricing-table aesthetics

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over noise.** Every visual element should push the reader toward the content, not decorate around it.
2. **Earned authority.** The design should feel like a briefing document, not a sports broadcast. Restraint communicates credibility.
3. **Typography carries the weight.** This is a writing-first site. Type hierarchy, measure, and rhythm matter more than graphic decoration.
4. **Confident color.** Use color intentionally and sparingly — accent where it communicates, nowhere else.
5. **No filler.** If a UI element doesn't do work, it doesn't appear. Empty states are honest, not dressed up.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High contrast in dark theme — text never below 4.5:1. Reduced motion respected via prefers-reduced-motion. No information conveyed by color alone.
